The Program

What MindSystem
Does For Your Family.

This is a mirror. Your daughter does her work. You do yours. The system connects them.

01

Shared Language

Your daughter learns the MAZE framework, Mindset, Analyze, Zero In, Execute, through her Athlete Compass Journal. You learn the same framework through your Family Alignment Guide. When she says she lost her reset after the second set, or that she used her verbal cue in the third, you know exactly what she means, because you're reading from the same playbook. You stop translating, guessing, or lecturing. You start responding from the same system she's already building.

02

Shared Definition of Success

At the start of the season, you and your daughter each walk through Walking the Maze, the onboarding ritual where you both define, in your own words, what success actually looks like before a single game is played. The system shows you where those definitions line up and where they don't. Most parents are measuring success differently than their kid and don't know it. That mismatch is where most car-ride-home arguments start. This fixes it before the season does the damage.

03

Shared System for Tracking Progress

Every week, you're both logging wins in the same place, not outcome wins, process wins. Hers might read: "I used my verbal cue in the third set." Yours might read: "I stayed quiet on the car ride home and she opened up on her own." You see what she's working on. She sees what you're working on. You stop managing her from the outside and start supporting her from alongside. That shift, from manager to collaborator, is what changes everything.

The Curriculum

The Journal Is The System.

Every athlete gets the Athlete Compass Journal, built to live in her bag all season. It starts with her goals, not yours. In the Mindset phase, she defines her why and writes an actual, actionable mantra, not an empty affirmation.

From there she works the rest of the MAZE model week by week: Analyze, where she connects her habits, sleep, focus, the small stuff, to what actually shows up on the court. Zero In, where she picks her three focus areas for the season. And Execute, where she runs two rituals all season long: the Compass Check before every competitive moment and the Debrief after. Both are hers, while your role is to support the process without taking it over.

Every parent gets the Family Alignment Guide, the mirror to her journal. You track your side: how you're supporting her, what you're noticing, where you're tempted to step in and where you're learning to step back. You're not tracking her. You're tracking your role in her process.

This is where the shift happens. You go from managing her season to collaborating on it. She owns her goals. You own your role. The journal connects them without you having to chase, question, or coach from the car.

The Family Journal

What Parents Receive.

Every family receives the Family Alignment Guide, a physical journal built for the parent side of this system. It's the mirror to your daughter's Athlete Compass Journal.

The research is clear: progress stalls when parents and athletes are operating with different definitions of success. The Family Alignment Guide gives parents a shared language, a shared picture of what progress actually looks like, and a shared system for tracking that progress alongside your athlete, week to week, through the whole season. It's built around the Mirror Moment, the parent practice of creating space between the emotion and the reaction, so you respond instead of react, and it runs on the same Walking the Maze and Win Log rituals your daughter is using in hers.

The moment a parent has a name for what they're watching, they stop panicking about the parts they can't control. That's what this journal does.

Objection Handling

This Works
Even If...

You're not betting on hope. You're betting on a system that works in the exact situations parents worry about most.

01 · Resistance

My daughter isn't on board.

The journal isn't homework. It's a private space, hers, not yours. She writes for herself, not for you.

02 · Starting Point

You're new to volleyball.

You don't need to arrive knowing the language. The system gives you and your daughter a clear place to begin together.

03 · Environment

The club environment is toxic.

You can't control every environment. You can give your family a process that stays steady inside it.

04 · Partnership

You and your partner aren't aligned.

The family guide creates a shared language for the adults supporting her, too.

05 · Parent Role

You don't know your role.

This is built for the parent learning when to step in, when to step back, and how to support the mental toll of uncertainty without putting it on her.

06 · The Reset

You've already tried everything.

If your daughter is doing the work but you're operating from a different playbook, it doesn't stick. This is built for the family.

The Timing

Do This During
High School Season.

Melanie Greene, former head coach at UC San Diego, ran this system with her team in the spring. By the time competitive season started, her athletes weren't learning the framework anymore. They were living it. The shared language was already there. The reset routines were automatic. The journal work was done.

That's the window right now. High school season is the runway. If your daughter's team starts this now, by the time club season hits, this isn't a program they're doing, it's the system they operate from.

The teams that wait until club season to address the mental game are already behind. They're building the foundation during the part of the year that matters most, instead of during the part of the year that's actually built for it.
