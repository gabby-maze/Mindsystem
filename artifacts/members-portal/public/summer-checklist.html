The Thing Most Families Miss

Every physical skill has a mental side.
Are you training both?

You're already handling the physical side. Private lessons. Camps. Conditioning. Touches outside of practice. You're investing in the game everyone can see.

But here's what most families don't realize: every single one of those physical reps has a mental component that is either helping or hurting the result.

If she's doing the physical work without training the intangible side, those reps aren't reaching their full potential. She could get more out of the same hours, without burning out her body further, just by also training what's happening between the ears.

That's the goal here. Not more work. Better work, because both sides of the game are being developed at the same time.

The Intangible Game What's usually missing
Practice composure under pressure in a low-stakes setting (a camp, an open gym, not a tryout)
Notice her self-talk after a mistake: name it, don't fix it yet
Role-play receiving hard coach feedback without getting defensive
Build a between-points reset routine now, while nothing's on the line

The Convergence Check
By the time the season starts, can she name her go-to reset routine without thinking?
Does she have language for what she's feeling instead of just reacting to it?
Has she practiced the hard conversation with a coach before she's actually in it?

If you can check those three at the top of the season, the physical work and the inner work converge. That's when she takes off.
