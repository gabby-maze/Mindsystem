Case Study

From Walking Away to a Real Development Plan.

A first-year club athlete. A tryout that went wrong. A family hours away from switching clubs. This is what happened next.

The message that started it
Actual parent message · identifying details removed
The Situation

A first-year club athlete, 13U, came out of rec ball into a competitive club environment. Tryouts happened. She didn't make a single travel team — offered only the developmental, non-travel roster. She was on the third-level team the year before.

The Spiral — Day 1

Within hours, the family was ready to leave the club entirely. The read: the coach didn't care, favoritism decided the rosters, their daughter was clearly better than the girls who moved up. A decision to switch clubs was days away from being made — based entirely on the emotion of the moment.

The Intervention
  • Paused the family before any decision was made — one week, no exceptions.
  • Had them reach out directly to the former coach for real feedback, not assumptions. They resisted. They did it anyway.
  • Ran the athlete through a guided emotional processing exercise to get past the surface reaction to the real one.
  • Reviewed film with the family to separate what they felt from what was actually happening technically.
The Turn

The coach responded in five minutes with real insight the family didn't have — and information that reframed the entire tryout result. Film review surfaced the actual development gap: strong ball control, but a game-IQ gap consistent with a first-year club athlete.

Not a favoritism story. A development story.

The Outcome

The follow-up call was built around game film, not just conversation. Body language everyone had been reading as low effort turned out to be something else entirely — positional confusion. She didn't know where she was supposed to be, so she was watching her feet instead of tracking the ball. That single reframe changed everything downstream.

A real development list came out of the session: footwork and stance, ball tracking, serve-receive timing, the mechanical difference between defense and serve-receive, out-of-system ball handling, and owning the position as the back-row quarterback. Private training shifted from solo reps to small groups built to simulate actual in-game pressure — because solo practice builds skill in a vacuum, not the read-and-react speed a real rotation requires.

The current team was confirmed as the right fit. She needs the reps more than she needs a different name on the jersey.

The pattern that mattered most: the same "they" language that showed up in the parents' first message — they didn't see her, they played favorites — was showing up in the athlete's own self-talk almost word for word. Coaching now targets both sides of that pattern together. Parents were coached on specific shifts: replacing comparison prompts with a chosen role model, reframing "if you get demoted" into "here's what it takes to make the team."

"This is what training the intangibles actually looks like end to end — not a single intervention, but a full season-long system that turns a tryout spiral into a real development plan. For the athlete and the parents both."
